\section{Introduction}

The growing quality and accessibility of face-manipulation tools have made it increasingly difficult to distinguish authentic media from manipulated content by visual inspection alone. This creates practical concerns for digital forensics, journalism, and online platforms. Research datasets such as FaceForensics++ and Celeb-DF have helped make the problem measurable and have enabled systematic comparison of detection methods \cite{rossler2019faceforensics,li2020celebdf}.

Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are a natural approach to this task because they can learn visual representations directly from image data. However, a detector's measured performance can depend on the manipulation methods, data split, compression, and evaluation protocol. Results on one dataset should therefore not be treated as proof of generalization to unseen sources.
